Stay safe

You can't stop every message arriving. You can make a clicking mistake much less costly.

Habits worth building

  1. Slow down on anything urgent. Pressure is the attacker's main tool, so treat it as a warning sign on its own.
  2. Go to the site yourself. Use a bookmark or type the address instead of following the link in the message.
  3. Hover before you click. On a computer, the real destination appears when you hover. On a phone, press and hold the link.
  4. Use a password manager. It only fills in passwords on the genuine site, which quietly catches fake pages.
  5. Turn on multi-factor authentication. Prefer an authenticator app or security key over text messages. Never read a code to someone who called you.
  6. Verify requests by a second route. If your boss emails about money, ring them on a number you already had.
  7. Keep software updated. It closes the holes that booby-trapped attachments rely on.
  8. Report what you see. Your report may protect colleagues who got the same email.

If you already clicked

It happens to everyone eventually. Speed and honesty matter more than embarrassment.

  1. Stop. Don't enter anything else, and close the page.
  2. If you typed a password, change it right away on the real website, using a different device if you can.
  3. If you downloaded or opened a file, disconnect from the network and tell your IT team.
  4. Report the message to IT, your manager or the service it pretended to be.
  5. Check your accounts for unfamiliar sign-ins, forwarding rules or payments.
  6. If money moved, contact your bank immediately and ask about stopping or recalling the payment.

A good rule of thumb: the more a message wants you to hurry, keep quiet or avoid your usual process, the more reason you have to stop and check.
