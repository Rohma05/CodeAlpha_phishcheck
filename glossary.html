Glossary

The words you'll see in news stories and security emails, without the jargon.

2FA / MFA
Two-factor or multi-factor authentication. A second proof of identity, such as an app code or a security key, on top of your password.
Attachment
A file sent along with an email. Common vehicles for malware, especially .exe, .zip and macro-enabled Office files.
Business email compromise
Fraud where an attacker poses as an executive or supplier to get money or data. Often just called BEC.
Domain
The main part of a web or email address, for example northfieldlibrary.org. Attackers register lookalikes of real ones.
Credential harvesting
Collecting usernames and passwords, usually through fake login pages.
Malware
Software built to cause harm, such as spyware, ransomware or tools that give an attacker remote control.
MFA fatigue
Spamming someone with approval prompts until they accept one just to make it stop.
Pharming
Sending people to a fake website even when they type the right address, by tampering with how the address is looked up.
Phishing
Tricking someone into handing over information or taking an action by pretending to be someone trustworthy.
Quishing
Phishing through a QR code, for example a sticker placed over a real one.
Ransomware
Malware that locks your files and demands payment to unlock them.
Smishing
Phishing sent by text message (SMS).
Social engineering
Manipulating people rather than computers. Phishing is the best-known kind.
Spear phishing
A targeted attack written for one person or small group using details about them.
Spoofing
Faking the sender name, address or caller ID so a message appears to come from someone else.
Vishing
Phishing by voice call.
Zero-day
A software flaw the maker doesn't yet know about, so no fix exists when attackers start using it.
